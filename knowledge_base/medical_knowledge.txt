PNEUMONIA

Definition:
Pneumonia is an infection of the lung parenchyma that can cause inflammation and impaired gas exchange.

Common clinical features:
- Fever or chills
- Cough, which may be productive or nonproductive
- Shortness of breath
- Pleuritic chest pain
- Fatigue or weakness
- Tachypnea may occur
- Hypoxemia may occur in more severe disease

Features that can support pneumonia:
- Fever associated with respiratory symptoms
- Cough with or without sputum production
- Focal respiratory findings on examination
- Chest imaging showing an infiltrate can support the diagnosis

Important considerations:
Pneumonia can range from mild illness to severe infection. Severity should be assessed using the patient's overall clinical condition, vital signs, oxygenation and relevant investigations.

ACUTE CORONARY SYNDROME AND MYOCARDIAL INFARCTION

Definition:
Acute coronary syndrome (ACS) refers to conditions caused by acute myocardial ischemia and includes unstable angina and myocardial infarction.

Common clinical features:
- Chest pressure, tightness, heaviness or discomfort
- Pain may radiate to the arm, shoulder, neck, jaw or back
- Shortness of breath
- Sweating
- Nausea or vomiting
- Weakness or lightheadedness

Important considerations:
Chest discomfort should not be assumed to be cardiac or non-cardiac based on symptoms alone. ACS requires appropriate clinical assessment and investigations.

Evaluation may include:
- 12-lead ECG
- Cardiac troponin testing
- Vital signs
- Clinical history and physical examination
- Additional testing according to clinical presentation

Red flag:
New or severe chest pain, particularly when associated with shortness of breath, sweating, fainting or other concerning symptoms, requires urgent medical assessment.

PULMONARY EMBOLISM

Definition:
Pulmonary embolism (PE) occurs when a blood clot, usually originating from the venous circulation, travels to the pulmonary arteries.

Common clinical features:
- Sudden or unexplained shortness of breath
- Pleuritic chest pain
- Tachycardia
- Cough
- Hemoptysis may occur
- Lightheadedness or syncope may occur in severe cases

Risk factors may include:
- Recent surgery or hospitalization
- Prolonged immobility
- Previous venous thromboembolism
- Active cancer
- Pregnancy or the postpartum period
- Estrogen exposure
- Certain inherited or acquired thrombophilic conditions

Important considerations:
PE can be life-threatening and may present with nonspecific symptoms. Clinical probability assessment and appropriate diagnostic testing are required.

Red flags:
Severe shortness of breath, syncope, hypotension, marked tachycardia or significant oxygen desaturation require urgent evaluation.

PLEURISY

Definition:
Pleurisy is inflammation of the pleura and typically produces pain that is worsened by breathing or coughing.

Common clinical features:
- Sharp chest pain
- Pain worsened by deep inspiration
- Pain worsened by coughing
- Pain may be localized to one side of the chest

Potential causes include:
- Viral infection
- Pneumonia
- Pulmonary embolism
- Other inflammatory or pulmonary conditions

Important considerations:
Pleuritic chest pain is a symptom rather than a specific diagnosis. Serious causes such as pulmonary embolism and pneumonia must be considered when supported by the clinical presentation.

==================================================

FEVER

Definition:
Fever is an elevation of body temperature associated with a regulated change in the body's temperature set point, commonly occurring during infection or inflammation.

Common associated features:
- Chills
- Sweating
- Fatigue
- Headache
- Muscle aches
- Increased heart rate may occur

Clinical considerations:
The cause of fever should be evaluated using the duration of illness, associated symptoms, examination findings and relevant investigations.

Fever with chest pain or breathing difficulty requires consideration of potentially serious cardiopulmonary causes.

CHEST PAIN

Chest pain has many possible causes and may originate from cardiovascular, pulmonary, gastrointestinal, musculoskeletal or other conditions.

Potential serious causes include:
- Acute coronary syndrome
- Pulmonary embolism
- Aortic emergencies
- Pneumothorax
- Severe pulmonary infection

Important clinical features to assess:
- Onset and duration
- Location
- Character of pain
- Severity
- Radiation
- Relationship to exertion
- Relationship to breathing or coughing
- Associated shortness of breath
- Sweating
- Nausea
- Syncope
- Relevant cardiovascular and thromboembolic risk factors

Important principle:
Chest pain should not be diagnosed from a single symptom. The differential diagnosis should be guided by the complete clinical presentation and appropriate evaluation.

DYSPNEA AND DIFFICULTY BREATHING

Definition:
Dyspnea is the subjective sensation of difficult or uncomfortable breathing.

Potential causes include:
- Pneumonia
- Pulmonary embolism
- Acute coronary syndrome
- Asthma
- Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease exacerbation
- Heart failure
- Pneumothorax
- Other pulmonary or cardiovascular conditions

Important clinical features:
- Sudden versus gradual onset
- Severity and progression
- Oxygen saturation
- Respiratory rate
- Chest pain
- Cough
- Fever
- Wheezing
- Syncope or altered mental status

Red flags:
Severe respiratory distress, cyanosis, altered mental status, severe hypoxemia, inability to speak normally because of breathlessness or hemodynamic instability require urgent medical evaluation.

==================================================

FEVER WITH CHEST PAIN AND DYSPNEA

Clinical reasoning:
The combination of fever, chest pain and difficulty breathing has a broad differential diagnosis.

Important possibilities include:
- Pneumonia
- Pulmonary embolism
- Acute coronary syndrome or myocardial infarction
- Pleurisy
- Other cardiopulmonary conditions

Fever may increase suspicion for an infectious process such as pneumonia, but fever does not exclude serious cardiovascular or thromboembolic disease.

Pleuritic chest pain may occur with pneumonia, pleurisy or pulmonary embolism.

Clinical assessment should consider:
- Onset and duration
- Vital signs
- Oxygen saturation
- Character and location of chest pain
- Cough and sputum
- Cardiovascular symptoms
- Thromboembolic risk factors
- Physical examination findings
- Appropriate laboratory and imaging investigations

Important principle:
The symptom combination alone is insufficient to establish a definitive diagnosis.

==================================================

CLINICAL RED FLAGS

Symptoms requiring urgent medical assessment may include:

- Severe or rapidly worsening difficulty breathing
- Severe or new chest pain
- Chest pain associated with sweating, syncope or severe weakness
- Fainting or near-fainting
- Cyanosis
- Severe oxygen desaturation
- Altered mental status
- Hypotension or signs of shock
- Severe tachycardia
- Rapid clinical deterioration

Clinical principle:
A clinical reasoning system should identify potentially life-threatening conditions and recommend urgent professional evaluation when red flags are present.

==================================================

DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS PRINCIPLES

Differential diagnosis is the process of considering multiple possible explanations for a patient's presentation.

A clinical reasoning system should:
- Identify the major presenting symptoms
- Consider multiple plausible conditions
- Identify findings that support each possibility
- Identify findings that argue against each possibility when available
- Consider important risk factors
- Identify potentially life-threatening diagnoses that should not be missed
- Distinguish between a possible diagnosis and a confirmed diagnosis
- Identify when additional clinical evaluation or testing is required

Important principle:
Symptoms alone generally cannot establish a definitive diagnosis. Clinical history, examination, vital signs and appropriate diagnostic investigations may be necessary.
